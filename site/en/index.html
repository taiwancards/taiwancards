Your all-in-one hub for Taiwanese Mandarin

Not Mandarin from China, converted into traditional characters. No vocabulary from China, Hong Kong, Singapore, or Malaysia. Zhuyin (ㄅㄆㄇㄈ) throughout — Taiwan's own phonetic script. Only Taiwan readings, Taiwan usage, Taiwan norms. Everything here is pure Taiwan.

Every word, sentence, and situation comes from real life in Taiwan: convenience store, tea shop, noodle shop, MRT, clinic, typhoon day off.

For people starting from zero, already studying, about to move to Taiwan, or already living here.

Behind it: quantitative linguistics, verified sources, my own teaching method, a great deal of work by hand, and a boundless love for Taiwan 🇹🇼

What this is

  • ✓Taiwan's Mandarin and nothing else — traditional characters, zhuyin, TOCFL bands and Taiwanese school grades
  • ✓Like Anki, only without decks by strangers or evenings lost to typing your own — and a word here is five separate memories, not one know / don't-know swipe
  • ✓Like Pleco, only it is not just a reference — the same entry feeds the trainers and a schedule that decides what you see next, with Taiwan first rather than as a footnote
  • ✓Like Skritter, only writing is not a separate app with a separate list — you draw the characters you are already learning this week, and it counts toward the same progress
  • ✓Like Du Chinese, only nobody picks the text for you: a song you love, a news piece, a class handout or a message — paste it and get it back split into words, ready to become a deck
  • ✓soonPhotograph a menu on the wall or a sign in the street, and the characters you could not read come back split into words, ready to become a deck
  • ✓Not a recogniser answering yes or no — every syllable you say is scored for quality on nineteen acoustic properties, against the way Taiwan says it rather than the way China does
  • ✓The Taiwan no course teaches: ordering at a drink shop, the invoice lottery, renting a flat, the clinic, typhoon days
  • ✓Every example sentence is one somebody in Taiwan actually wrote, kept together with its source and its licence
  • ✓Nothing here is guessed: how common a word is, how hard it is and which level it belongs to all come from counting a real Taiwanese corpus
  • ✓One progress record behind every screen: learn a word in the reader and it counts in the dictionary, the drills and the writing trainer

What this is not

  • ✕Not a game — no streaks, no XP, no badges, no leaderboards and no cartoon animal guilt-tripping you into coming back tomorrow
  • ✕No AI content — and there never will be. Every meaning, example and label has passed through a person, and every phrase traces back to a verifiable source. Most apps show you what a model generated; this one never does
  • ✕Not a pile of shared decks where nobody can tell you who made them, from which textbook, or whether the readings are even right for Taiwan
  • ✕Not a course from China with traditional characters swapped in — the vocabulary, the readings and half the everyday realities differ as well
  • ✕A course from China is unlikely to spend much time on 自由, 民主 or 人權. Here there is no shortage of words like that, and no list they are missing from
  • ✕Not a syllabus to sit through from the first lesson to the last — you start where you already are, take what you need and skip what you do not
  • ✕Not a replacement for a teacher — this is the half you do between classes, the half nobody watches you do, and the half that decides whether you get anywhere
  • ✕Not HSK preparation — different vocabulary, different readings and different realities behind the words, even if you sit the exam in traditional characters
  • ✕No simplified characters anywhere and no switch to turn them on — there is only ever the Taiwan side, in the dictionary, the examples and the trainers alike
  • ✕Not funded by advertising and never will be — nothing flashes, nothing interrupts, nothing pulls you away from studying or from simply looking a word up
  • ✕Not watching you — no third-party analytics, no trackers of any kind, and your whole progress exports to a file you keep whenever you ask for it

Written for an English speaker

Quantitative linguistics under the hood

Everything rests on measurement: corpus frequencies and dispersion, collocation strength, calibrated difficulty scales, acoustic references for every syllable. What you see is the tip of an iceberg — beneath it are years of collecting, measuring and verifying data, with our own libraries and datasets released for the research community. All of it is open — knowledge belongs to everyone.

Measured, not guessed

Levels, difficulty and word order come out of a Taiwan corpus we built and measured ourselves — frequency, dispersion, collocation strength, register.

Open to inspection

The corpus tables, the syllable inventory and the libraries behind them are published as datasets, and this application is open source. Every source and licence is on the credits page.

Next to what you may already be using

Each of those apps does its own job well. The trouble is keeping five of them open with nothing joined up in between.

Look something up, check it, send it to a deck, watch your level rise and prepare for TOCFL — in one place rather than five tabs.

It will not prepare you for HSK: the vocabulary and the realities behind it are different, even if you sit the exam in traditional characters.

Show the full comparison table

Apps that teach Mandarin nearly always teach the Chinese one, and the strong general-purpose tools leave the hard choices to you: which deck, which word next, how often. TaiwanCards is narrower on purpose, and makes those choices itself.

None of that makes the others bad software, and most of them are not trying to teach Taiwanese Mandarin at all. Anki's ecosystem is unmatched. Pleco's dictionaries are far deeper than anything here. Skritter's stroke-level handwriting grading is the best in the category. And Duolingo is better than anyone at getting you back tomorrow. Zhong Chinese, newly out of Taipei, is the one other place here teaching the Mandarin of Taiwan rather than the mainland one, and it is a pleasure to look at — its material follows a single textbook and its dictionary is small, but what is in it is the real thing. 嗨嗨 👋🏻

Your teacher's right hand

Phonetics is best set up with a live teacher, and this does not replace real classes. What you get is tone and pronunciation checking — there is no conversation partner and no listening comprehension here, and the app does not set out to provide them.

Why this exists

I am a linguist, I live in Taiwan, and this is the app I kept wishing for while learning the language here. It grew out of a master's thesis in computational linguistics and a lot of ordinary daily practice — signs, menus, asking again when I miss it. There is no advertising and there never will be, and that is half the reason it exists.
